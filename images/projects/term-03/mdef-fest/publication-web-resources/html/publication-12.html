Chapter II

Monster Cartographies: Reading Culture Through the Stack

Earth is where extraction happens. The coltan mines of Congo, the lithium fields of Bolivia, the deforested territories of the Amazon — these are the sacrifice zones of computation. The monster here is colonial: the body and the land treated as resource, as raw material, as disposable. Every algorithm has a geology.

Cloud is where data colonialism operates. The server farms of Virginia, Dublin, Singapore — non-places that exercise sovereign power over the bodies they classify. The monster here is invisible, systemic, incorporeal. It decides your credit score. It flags your face at the border. It amplifies your rage until it becomes someone else’s violence.

City is where the crisis becomes visible in urban form. Pandemic governance failure in São Paulo. Class warfare made architectural in Seoul. The smart city as surveillance infrastructure. The monster here is the system that was supposed to protect you — revealing, under pressure, who it was actually built for.Address is where identity becomes a coordinate — where a body is assigned a legal existence or denied one. The refugee camp. The detention center. The biometric checkpoint.

Address is where the monster has no category becomes a juridical condition: you exist, but the system cannot process you. You are present but unclassifiable.

Interface is where the monster touches you. The screen, the filter, the platform — the surface where bodies are made legible, desirable, or disposable. The algorithm that smooths your face before you post it. The rating system that scores your femininity. Interface is where the monstrous feminine lives in its most contemporary form: not in the creature, but in the standard she is measured against.User is the final and most unstable layer.

User is the subject position the system produces: who gets to be recognized as a legal, human, classifiable person. The User is not a given. It is an assignment. And it can be revoked.
