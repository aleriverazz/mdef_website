b. The Borderlands as Laboratory: Where the Monster Lives Now

Tijuana — San Diego

There is a border I know from the inside.The Tijuana-San Diego border is not a line.

It is a liminal zone where six Stack layers collapse into one geography — and where every layer fails simultaneously.

At the Earth layer: Two cities built on the same topography, the same desert, the same watershed — divided by a wall that the land itself does not recognize. The scale model of this territory, printed in six tiles across the same terrain, makes visible what the political border tries to fix: geography does not stop where the system decides.

At the Cloud layer: CBP databases, facial recognition systems, predictive algorithms that decide threat levels before a body reaches the checkpoint. The data infrastructure of the border is vast, invisible, and wrong at a rate that ends lives.

At the City layer: Tijuana and San Diego are the same metropolitan region performing two different systems of urban life. Maquiladoras on one side. Tech campuses on the other. The same labor, different wages, different bodies.

At the Interface layer: the border is screened, surveilled, mediated. Drone footage. Body cameras. The interface through which the border is seen by those who will never cross it — and through which those who cross it are seen as data, not people.

At the User layer: who gets to be a User at this border? Who gets the credential, the document, the address that makes you legible to the system? The User here is not a person. It is a permission.

The uncanny valley, in this project, is geographic. The monster at the Tijuana-San Diego border is not the migrant. It is the classification system that cannot process a human being and calls that failure security.
