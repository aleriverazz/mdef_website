Every monster is a political decision.This is not a book about horror films. It is a book about what horror films reveal — about the systems that organize bodies, territories, and lives, and about the fractures those systems cannot hide.Monster Cartographies began with a simple question: what can we read from pop culture horror, and what does that reading tell us about the geopolitical world we actually live in? The answer, developed across two chapters and a critical diagram, is this: the monster does not create the crisis. It reveals where the classification system was always already failing.This essay moves between theory and cartography, between the digital diagram and the material print, between academic frameworks and the border I grew up on. It is a work in progress — a methodology under construction, a map that is still being drawn.Chapter I establishes the genealogy of the monster: its migration from physical body to juridical anomaly, from action to place. Chapter II proposes the method: a cultural reading matrix that maps how pop culture horror intersects with geopolitical crises and the spatial layers of Benjamin Bratton’s Stack — producing a cartography of the places where classification fails.
